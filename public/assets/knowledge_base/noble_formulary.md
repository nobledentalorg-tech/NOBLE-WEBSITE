# Noble Dental Medication Options (Reference Only)

## Pain Management Logic
**Disclaimer:** Dosage depends on age, weight, and kidney function. Consult doctor.

### Level 1: Mild Pain (Sensitivity / Post-Filling)
* **Option:** Paracetamol 650mg.
* **Why:** Safest analgesic. No stomach irritation. Safe for most heart patients.

### Level 2: Severe Inflammatory Pain (Pulpitis / Surgery)
* **Option:** Ketorolac (NSAID).
* **Why:** Directly blocks the enzymes causing pain.
* **Warning:** Do NOT take if you have acidity/ulcers or Asthma.

### Level 3: Nerve Pain (Electric Shock Sensation)
* **Option:** Pregabalin + Methylcobalamin.
* **Why:** Calms the overactive nerve firing.
* **Warning:** Causes drowsiness. Do not drive.

## Antibiotic Logic
**Disclaimer:** Antibiotics do not cure tooth pain; they only stop spread of infection.

### First Line (Standard)
* **Option:** Amoxicillin 500mg.
* **Indication:** Swelling localized to the gum.

### Second Line (Severe/Resistant)
* **Option:** Amoxicillin + Clavulanic Acid (625mg).
* **Indication:** Swelling spreading to cheek/neck.

# Noble Dental Formulary: Detailed Monographs

## Drug: Amoxicillin (Antibiotic / Penicillin Class)

### 1. Mechanism of Action (MOA)
* **Target:** Penicillin-Binding Proteins (PBPs) inside the bacterial cell wall.
* **Action:** Inhibits cross-linkage of peptidoglycan polymer chains.
* **Result:** Weakens bacterial cell wall, causing cell lysis (bactericidal) during the multiplication stage.
* **Systemic Benefit:** reduces systemic inflammatory markers (CRP) by eliminating the bacterial source.

### 2. Therapeutic Indications
* **Primary:** Dentoalveolar abscess (acute).
* **Secondary:** Prophylaxis for infective endocarditis (2g single dose).
* **Off-Label:** Not recommended for viral sore throat.

### 3. Dosage Calculation & Therapeutic Window
* **Therapeutic Range:** 25mg/kg/day to 45mg/kg/day (divided in 2-3 doses).
* **Adult Standard:** 500mg every 8 hours (TID) OR 875mg every 12 hours (BID).
* **Pediatric Calculation (Example):**
    * *Weight:* 20kg
    * *Low Dose (20mg/kg):* 400mg/day.
    * *High Dose (40mg/kg):* 800mg/day.
* **Max Daily Dose:** 3000mg (3g).

### 4. Contraindications & Warnings
* **Absolute Contraindication:** History of Anaphylaxis to Penicillins or Cephalosporins (Cross-sensitivity 3-7%).
* **Warning (Renal):** If GFR < 30 ml/min, dose adjustment required (extend interval to q12h).
* **Warning (Hepatic):** Safe, metabolized minimally by liver.

### 5. Drug Cross-Interactions
* **Methotrexate:** Amoxicillin reduces renal clearance of Methotrexate -> **RISK:** Methotrexate Toxicity (Severe).
* **Oral Contraceptives:** May reduce efficacy -> **ADVICE:** Use barrier method for 7 days.
* **Allopurinol:** Increases incidence of skin rash (Non-allergic) -> **RISK:** Moderate.
* **Warfarin:** Increases INR (bleeding risk) -> **ADVICE:** Monitor PT/INR.

### 6. Side Effects Profile
* **Mild (Common):** Nausea, Diarrhea, Black Hairy Tongue.
* **Moderate (Monitor):** Urticaria (Hives), Oral Candidiasis (Thrush).
* **Severe (Stop Immediately):** Anaphylaxis (Angioedema, Wheezing), C. Difficile Colitis (Bloody diarrhea), Stevens-Johnson Syndrome.

## Drug: Ketorolac (NSAID / Analgesic)

### 1. Mechanism of Action (MOA)
* **Target:** Cyclooxygenase (COX-1 and COX-2) enzymes.
* **Action:** Potent inhibition of prostaglandin synthesis.
* **Result:** Strong Analgesic (Pain Relief) comparable to opioids, but with anti-inflammatory properties.
* **Systemic Benefit:** Rapid onset of action for acute dental pain management.

### 2. Therapeutic Indications
* **Primary:** Short-term management of moderate to severe acute dental pain (e.g., Post-Extraction, Pulpitis).
* **Limit:** Not for chronic pain. Max duration 5 days.

### 3. Dosage Calculation & Therapeutic Window
* **Adult Standard:** 10mg every 4-6 hours as needed for pain.
* **Max Daily Dose:** 40mg/day.
* **Pediatric:** Not generally recommended for children for routine dental pain without specialist oversight.

### 4. Contraindications & Warnings
* **Absolute Contraindication:** Active Peptic Ulcer Disease, GI Bleeding, Advanced Renal Impairment, History of Asthma (Aspirin-sensitive).
* **Warning (Bleeding):** Inhibits platelet function. Avoid pre-surgery.
* **Warning (Renal):** Can cause acute renal failure in dehydrated patients.

### 5. Drug Cross-Interactions
* **Other NSAIDs (Ibuprofen, Aspirin):** Increases risk of GI ulcers/bleeding -> **RISK:** Severe.
* **ACE Inhibitors:** Reduced antihypertensive effect + Renal risk -> **RISK:** Moderate.
* **Anticoagulants (Warfarin):** Significantly increases bleeding risk -> **RISK:** Severe.

### 6. Side Effects Profile
* **Mild (Common):** Nausea, Abdominal pain, Drowsiness.
* **Moderate (Monitor):** Gastritis, edema.
* **Severe (Stop Immediately):** GI Bleeding (Black stool), Acute Renal Failure, Anaphylaxis.
