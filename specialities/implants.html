Medical Evaluation We Do (Simple & Safe)

We coordinate with your physician when needed, aligning with ITI/ADA/AAOMS guidance.

Diabetes

  • We request recent HbA1c; typically aim ≤ ~7–7.5%.
  • Antibiotic & healing protocols tailored to risk.
  • Uncontrolled → stabilise first.

Hypertension & Cardiac

  • We check BP; adjust anaesthetic as needed.
  • Recent MI/stroke (≤ 6 months) → defer & clearance.

Bleeding & Medications

  • Anticoagulants/antiplatelets: physician plan + local haemostasis.
  • High-dose IV antiresorptives: discuss alternatives.

PRF (Platelet-Rich Fibrin) — Your Biology, Your Boost

What it is

A small blood draw is spun to concentrate platelets and growth factors into a fibrin mesh used around the surgical site.

Why we use it

May improve soft-tissue healing and graft handling. It’s autologous (from you), reducing reaction risks.

When

Common in grafts, sinus lifts, immediate implants and thin soft-tissue biotypes.

Bone Grafting & Sinus Lift — When We Need More Bone

Minor grafts

  • Particulate or block graft to thicken ridge walls.
  • Often alongside implant placement.

Sinus lift (lateral/internal)

  • Raises sinus floor to gain height for upper back implants.
  • PRF/membranes used for stability in many cases.

Materials

  • Autograft (your bone), allograft, xenograft, alloplast.
  • We select based on site & evidence.

Questions about this step
Why use a surgical guide?
Guides transfer the digital plan to your mouth, improving precision and reducing surgery time.
Is a guide always required?
Not always, but we recommend it for complex angulations, immediate implants, or proximity to nerves/sinus.

Immediate Implant + PRF

Extraction and implant in one sitting with PRF membranes for soft-tissue support. Ideal when insertion torque is adequate.

Questions about PRF
Does PRF improve healing?
PRF concentrates your platelets, releasing growth factors that may support faster soft-tissue healing and graft stability.
Is PRF safe?
Yes. It’s derived from your own blood (autologous), so the risk of reaction is minimal.

Sinus Lift Overview

Animated explanation of internal vs lateral sinus lift and how grafts create safe space for implants away from the sinus.

Questions about sinus lift
Why is a sinus lift needed?
When upper back jaw bone is too short after tooth loss, lifting the sinus floor adds height for stable implants.
How long is healing after sinus lift?
Typically 4–6 months before final loading; varies with graft volume and bone quality.

Follow-Ups & Longevity

Immediate

  • Day 0–2: cold compress, soft diet, routine pain relief.
  • No smoking or vigorous rinsing for 48–72 hours.

Short-term

  • Sutures at 7–14 days.
  • Hygiene review; tailor brushes/floss aids.

Long-term

  • Reviews at 3–4 months (integration), then 6–12 months.
  • With good control, implants often last decades.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is “teeth in a day” always possible?
Only when insertion torque and bone quality permit. Otherwise, we allow routine healing (8–12 weeks) for predictable results.
Are zirconia implants better than titanium?
Titanium remains the most studied and versatile. Zirconia can be considered for metal-sensitive patients or specific esthetic demands; case selection is key.
What if I grind my teeth (bruxism)?
We often recommend a night guard, occlusal adjustments, and periodic screw checks to protect your work.
