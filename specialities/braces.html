How do braces actually move teeth?

Braces deliver light, continuous forces through brackets and shape-memory wires. They stimulate the ligament around each tooth, creating space on one side while bone fills in on the other. The result is a slow, safe remodelling of bone rather than "pushing" teeth.

1. Smart bonding

Brackets are positioned with digital guides so the wire sits exactly where your orthodontist wants the tooth to finish.

2. Nickel-titanium memory wires

Heat-activated wires want to return to a programmed shape, applying gentle forces even when teeth are crowded.

3. Biological response

Cells remove bone in the pressure area and deposit bone where the tooth is moving, keeping roots healthy.

4. Finishing & detailing

Stainless-steel wires fine tune bite, smile arc and midline with elastics, springs and finishing bends.

What you will feel

Expect gentle tightness for 2–3 days after an adjustment and then a sensation of teeth feeling slightly loose — that is bone remodelling in action.

  • Days 1–3: soft diet, OTC pain relief if required.
  • Days 4–21: teeth feel normal; focus on hygiene and elastic wear.
  • Any sharpness? Cover with wax and ping us on WhatsApp.

Monthly review routine

Every visit is mapped to the digital simulation so we only tighten when tissues are ready and you always know the next milestone.

  • Progress photos and iTero scan overlays.
  • Archwire change or elastic reconfiguration.
  • Quick polish + fluoride gel to keep enamel strong.

Why choose braces instead of clear aligners?

  • They excel at complex skeletal discrepancies needing elastics, bite turbos or growth modulation.
  • Braces stay active 24/7, so compliance is not a worry for younger teens.
  • They allow 3D control of tooth roots for rotation, torque and vertical correction.
  • Treatment cost is typically lower and repairs are simpler if an attachment breaks.

Limitations of clear aligners

  • Require 20–22 hours/day wear — lost trays can set progress back by weeks.
  • Less predictable for severe rotations, open bites or impacted teeth.
  • Attachments may be visible and needs interproximal polishing.
  • Complex cases demand elastic wear plus braces finishing later.

Deep bite with crowding

Braces: Torque control and bite turbos open the bite while roots are positioned upright.

Aligners struggle when: Severe rotations need attachments on almost every tooth and mid-course refinements prolong wear.

Impacted canine or missing teeth

Braces: Hooks, chains and TADs (mini-screws) guide buried teeth into position with precision.

Aligners struggle when: Space opening and traction forces exceed tray flexibility leading to stalled movement.

Growth modulation

Braces: Pair seamlessly with functional appliances to harness remaining jaw growth.

Aligners struggle when: Limited ability to couple with orthopedic devices or deliver 24/7 mandibular guidance.

Who can start braces at Noble Dental Care?

Orthodontics isn't just for teenagers. We customise protocols for growing kids, young professionals and adults seeking bite rehabilitation.

Age 7–11 · Interceptive phase

Early screening lets us guide jaw growth, expand arches and make room for permanent teeth — best time to guide habits and breathing.

Age 12–17 · Growth spurt advantage

The pubertal growth spurt is ideal to correct skeletal imbalances with functional appliances, reducing need for future surgery.

18+ · Adult orthodontics

We coordinate periodontal care, restorative dentistry and braces to align teeth safely at any age with CBCT planning.

Best time to start?

Growth spurts make skeletal corrections easier, yet there is no upper age limit — we treat motivated adults in their 40s, 50s and beyond once gums are stable.

  • 7–11 years: guide jaw growth and habits.
  • 12–17 years: harness pubertal growth for skeletal balance.
  • 18+: align safely with periodontal and restorative support.

Ideal candidates

  • Healthy gums and supporting bone
  • Willing to attend monthly adjustments
  • Motivated to follow oral hygiene & diet guidance
  • Growth potential (for skeletally-driven cases)
  • No upper age limit if periodontal status is stable

Who should wait?

  • Active tooth decay or gum infection — needs stabilisation first
  • Untreated jaw fractures or severe bone loss
  • Medications affecting bone metabolism (consult physician)
  • Pregnancy first trimester — we plan, then begin after clearance
  • Acute TMJ pain or clenching — manage symptoms before starting

Contra-indications

  • Recent jaw or facial fractures
  • Uncontrolled diabetes or bleeding disorders
  • Severe periodontal disease with mobility
  • Allergy to nickel without alternative bracket options
  • Poor oral hygiene compliance despite coaching

Pre-braces readiness checklist

  • Complete fillings or extractions recommended by your dentist.
  • Schedule a professional cleaning and polish.
  • Share medical history, medications and recent reports.

Lifestyle adjustments

  • Switch to a soft-bristled brush and fluoride paste.
  • Collect orthodontic wax, interdental brushes and a travel kit.
  • Discuss school, travel or wedding timelines so we pace treatment right.

Understanding malocclusions in friendly language

We categorise issues by the bones (skeletal) and the teeth (dental). Tap each to jump into a simple explanation.

Skeletal class II Skeletal class III Dental crowding Spacing & diastema Open bite Crossbite

Skeletal Class II (upper jaw ahead)

Your upper jaw has grown a little ahead or the lower jaw is set back. During growth we use twin-blocks or Herbst devices with braces to nudge the jaw forward. Adults can pair braces with jaw advancement surgery.

Skeletal Class III (underbite)

The lower jaw is ahead of the upper jaw. Early facemask therapy during growth helps pull the upper jaw forward. Adults may need surgical orthodontics with braces to align the bite.

Dental crowding

Teeth jostle for space because the arch is narrow. We expand arches, strip tiny enamel widths or remove teeth only if necessary to align them neatly.

Spacing & diastema

Gaps between teeth from small tooth size or missing teeth. Braces close spaces and coordinate with implants or bonding to fill the smile evenly.

Open bite

Front teeth do not touch. We identify tongue posture or breathing concerns, then use braces with elastics, mini screws or aligners to bring the bite together.

Crossbite

One or more upper teeth bite inside lower teeth. Early expansion or cross-elastics prevent uneven jaw growth. Back to malocclusion list.

Skeletal problems relate to the jaw bones and often need growth guidance or surgical support, while dental problems focus on tooth positioning. Most smiles are a mix, so we explain the recipe for your case with visuals before bonding.

Daily care, do's & don'ts while wearing braces

Small habits keep brackets secure, gums healthy and treatment on schedule.

Do

  • Brush with a soft orthodontic brush morning & night
  • Rinse with fluoride mouthwash every evening
  • Use interdental brushes under the wire daily
  • Report poking wires or loose brackets immediately

Don't

  • Chew ice, hard candy or pen caps
  • Skip elastic wear if prescribed
  • Forget to wear sports mouthguard during games
  • Nibble sticky foods like caramel or gummy candy

Consistency beats perfection — if brackets pop off or gums feel puffy, message us immediately. Same-day fixes keep timelines intact.

Retainers: the insurance for your new smile

Teeth always try to drift back. Retainers are non-negotiable to protect the time and investment you put into braces.

Fixed lingual retainer

A slim wire bonded behind front teeth. Invisible, permanent and perfect for long-term stability.

Essix removable retainer

Clear trays worn nightly. We provide 2 sets and refresh them yearly.

Vivera / Custom night guard

For grinders or aligner transitions. 3D printed and shipped in sets of three.

First 6 months

Wear removable retainers nightly and visit us at 1, 3 and 6 months to ensure the bond is intact.

Months 7–12

Shift to alternate-night wear while we monitor bite settling and enamel health.

Yearly thereafter

Annual retention check with optional aligner refresh or whitening touch-up.

Retainers are mandatory — we include a 1-year retention programme with every braces plan.
